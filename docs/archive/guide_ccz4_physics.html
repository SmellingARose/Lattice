Last updated: 2026-02-17  |  Primary source: src/evolution/ccz4_rhs.c

1. What is CCZ4?

Einstein's equations in one sentence

General relativity says spacetime curvature equals energy-momentum content: G = 8 pi T. For vacuum (no matter), this simplifies to R = 0 — the Ricci tensor vanishes. The problem: these are 10 coupled nonlinear PDEs in 4 dimensions. You cannot evolve them forward in time directly because time and space are tangled together.

The 3+1 split

The first step is to separate time from space. Slice 4D spacetime into a stack of 3D spatial "snapshots" (hypersurfaces). Between slices, you need two pieces of information: the lapse (how much proper time elapses between slices) and the shift (how spatial coordinates slide sideways between slices). The 3D geometry of each slice is described by the spatial metric gamma and the extrinsic curvature K (how the slice bends in 4D). This is the ADM formalism (Arnowitt, Deser, Misner).

Why ADM is unstable

The ADM equations are only weakly hyperbolic. In practice, small numerical errors grow exponentially and crash the simulation within tens of timesteps. Every modern NR code uses a reformulation that is strongly hyperbolic.

BSSN: "split how big from what shape"

The BSSN (Baumgarte-Shapiro-Shibata-Nakamura) formulation factors the spatial metric into an overall scale factor chi and a unit-determinant "conformal" metric h_ij. Think of it as separating how big the geometry is (chi) from what shape it has (h_ij). Similarly, the extrinsic curvature is split into its trace K and a traceless conformal part A_ij. Three extra evolved variables (the conformal connection functions Gamma^i) are introduced to make the system strongly hyperbolic. This stabilizes the simulation dramatically.

CCZ4: cruise control for constraints

Even BSSN has a problem: Einstein's equations include constraint equations (the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints) that must hold on every time slice. Analytically they are preserved, but numerically they drift. BSSN does nothing to correct this drift.

CCZ4 (Conformal Covariant Z4) adds a constraint damping mechanism. An extra scalar variable Theta and a vector Z_i are introduced, and terms proportional to kappa1 and kappa2 actively push constraint violations back toward zero. The analogy: BSSN is a car rolling downhill with no brakes; CCZ4 is the same car with cruise control that corrects speed deviations. Lattice implements CCZ4 following arXiv:1106.2254.

Memory layout

Struct-of-Arrays (SoA) layout — each field is a contiguous double* array fields[0] = FIELD_CHI : [chi_000, chi_100, chi_200, ... ] x is unit-stride fields[1] = FIELD_H11 : [h11_000, h11_100, h11_200, ... ] fields[2] = FIELD_H12 : [h12_000, h12_100, h12_200, ... ] ... fields[24] = FIELD_B3 : [B3_000, B3_100, B3_200, ... ] Index: IDX(g, i, j, k) = i + Ntotal * (j + Ntotal * k) where Ntotal = N + 2 * ghost, ghost = 4 Loop order: k (outer) -> j -> i (inner, unit-stride in x) Allocations: page-aligned (4096 bytes) for GPU zero-copy

The field enum in src/core/fields.h is append-only — existing indices must never be reordered. Phase 2 will append E_i and B_i fields (6 more) for Einstein-Maxwell coupling.

3. The Evolution Equations

The core physics kernel is ccz4_rhs_point() in src/evolution/ccz4_rhs.c. It computes the time derivative of all 25 fields at a single grid point. The function proceeds through 11 computation steps.

ccz4_rhs_point() — 11 computation steps Step 1 Load fields into locals chi, h[3][3], K, A[3][3], Theta, Gamma[3], lapse, shift[3], B[3] Step 2 First derivatives (d1) d1_chi, d1_h, d1_A, d1_K, d1_Theta, d1_lapse, d1_Gamma, d1_shift Step 3 Second derivatives (d2) d2_chi, d2_h, d2_lapse, d2_shift (diagonal + mixed) Step 4 Advection derivatives beta^a * d_a(f) for every field, using upwind stencil | Step 5 Inverse metric + Christoffel h_UU[3][3], chris.ULL, chris.LLL, chris.contracted Step 6 Z vector Z_over_chi[i] = 0.5*(Gamma[i] - chris_contracted[i]) Step 7 Ricci tensor with Z terms ricci_hat + ricci_chi + z_terms --> ricci.LL, ricci.scalar Step 8 Covariant derivatives of lapse covd2lapse[3][3], tr_covd2lapse, A_UU, tr_A2 | Step 9 CCZ4 RHS: chi, h, K, A, Theta, Gamma Step 10 Moving puncture gauge: lapse, shift, B Step 11 Store RHS + Kreiss-Oliger dissipation
dt(chi) — conformal factor evolution

Conceptual formula: The conformal factor changes due to the overall expansion rate of the slice (K) minus the coordinate stretching (div shift).

dt(chi) = advection + (2/3) * chi * (alpha * K - div(shift))

When CAHD (Coarse-grid-Adjusted Hamiltonian Damping) is enabled, an additional term proportional to the Hamiltonian constraint H is added. This damps constraint violations near punctures on coarse grids.

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:288-302  |  Ref: GRChombo CCZ4RHS.impl.hpp:118-119

dt(h_ij) — conformal metric evolution

Conceptual formula: The shape of space evolves due to tidal distortion (A_ij) plus coordinate deformations (shift derivatives).

dt(h_ij) = advection - 2*alpha*A_ij - (2/3)*h_ij*div(shift)
         + h_{ki}*d_j(shift^k) + h_{kj}*d_i(shift^k)

The last two terms are the Lie derivative of the metric along the shift vector, accounting for coordinate motion.

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:307-316  |  Ref: GRChombo CCZ4RHS.impl.hpp:120-129

dt(K) — trace of extrinsic curvature

Conceptual formula: K evolves due to Ricci curvature, self-interaction (K squared), constraint damping (Theta), and the Laplacian of the lapse.

dt(K) = advection + alpha*(R + K*(K - 2*Theta))
      - kappa1*(1 + kappa2)*3*Theta - Laplacian(alpha)

The kappa1 * Theta term is the CCZ4 constraint damping: it pushes K back toward the constraint surface when Theta is nonzero.

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:359-363  |  Ref: GRChombo CCZ4RHS.impl.hpp:183-191

dt(A_ij) — traceless conformal extrinsic curvature

Conceptual formula: A_ij evolves due to the trace-free part of (Ricci - lapse Hessian), plus nonlinear A-squared terms and shift Lie derivative.

dt(A_ij) = advection + [-D_i D_j alpha + chi*alpha*R_ij]^TF
         + A_ij * alpha*(K - 2*Theta) - (2/3)*A_ij*div(shift)
         + Lie_shift(A_ij) - 2*alpha*A_{ik}*A^k_j

The TF superscript means "trace-free part" — the trace is removed using make_trace_free(). This keeps A_ij consistent with its definition as the traceless part of the extrinsic curvature.

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:321-340  |  Ref: GRChombo CCZ4RHS.impl.hpp:131-154

dt(Theta) — CCZ4 constraint damping scalar

Conceptual formula: Theta is driven toward zero by the kappa1 damping term. It also responds to the Hamiltonian constraint (R - A^2 + 2/3 K^2).

dt(Theta) = advection + 0.5*alpha*(R - A^2 + (2/3)*K^2 - 2*Theta*K)
          - 0.5*Theta*kappa1*(4 + kappa2*2) - Z . grad(alpha)

In a well-converged simulation, Theta stays close to zero. Large Theta values indicate constraint violation (a "code smell" for physics bugs).

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:344-356  |  Ref: GRChombo CCZ4RHS.impl.hpp:172-180

dt(Gamma^i) — conformal connection functions

Conceptual formula: The most complex equation. Gamma^i evolves due to shift second derivatives, A_ij gradients, chi gradients, Z vector damping, and interactions with Christoffel symbols.

dt(Gamma^i) = advection
  + (2/3)*div(shift)*(Gamma^i_contracted + 2*kappa3*Z/chi^i)
  - 2*alpha*K*Z/chi^i - 2*kappa1*alpha*Z/chi^i
  + 2*h^ij*(alpha*d_j Theta - Theta*d_j alpha)
  - 2*A^ij * d_j alpha
  - alpha*((4/3)*h^ij*d_j K + 3*A^ij*d_j chi / chi)
  + 2*alpha*Gamma^i_{jk}*A^{jk}
  + h^{jk}*d2_shift^i_{jk} + (1/3)*h^{ij}*d2_shift^k_{jk}
  - (Gamma^j_contracted + 2*kappa3*Z/chi^j)*d_j shift^i

This equation provides the strong hyperbolicity that makes the system stable. The Gamma^i variables track the contracted Christoffel symbols, and the Z vector correction ensures constraint damping propagates through the connection functions.

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:367-395  |  Ref: GRChombo CCZ4RHS.impl.hpp:193-222

4. Finite Differences

All spatial derivatives go through four inline functions in src/numerics/finite_diff.h. No hand-coded stencils are allowed anywhere else in the codebase.

fd_d1 — 4th-order first derivative

4th-order centered first derivative: d f / d x Stencil (5 points, 4 nonzero weights): i-2 i-1 i i+1 i+2 | | | | | +1/12 -2/3 0 +2/3 -1/12 | | | | | fd_d1(f, idx, stride, dx) = (+1/12*f[i-2] -2/3*f[i-1] +2/3*f[i+1] -1/12*f[i+2]) / dx Truncation error: O(dx^4). Doubling resolution cuts error by 16x. Source: finite_diff.h:24-31 | Ref: GRChombo FourthOrderDerivatives.hpp:36-46

fd_d2 — 4th-order second derivative

4th-order centered second derivative: d^2 f / d x^2 Stencil (5 points, all nonzero): i-2 i-1 i i+1 i+2 | | | | | -1/12 +4/3 -5/2 +4/3 -1/12 | | | | | fd_d2(f, idx, stride, dx) = (-1/12*f[i-2] +4/3*f[i-1] -5/2*f[i] +4/3*f[i+1] -1/12*f[i+2]) / dx^2 Source: finite_diff.h:37-45 | Ref: GRChombo FourthOrderDerivatives.hpp:114-128

fd_adv — 4th-order upwind advection

4th-order upwind advection: beta * d f / d x Stencil depends on sign of velocity beta: When beta > 0 (flow to the right), bias stencil left: i-1 i i+1 i+2 i+3 | | | | | -1/4 -5/6 +3/2 -1/2 +1/12 When beta < 0 (flow to the left), bias stencil right (mirror): i-3 i-2 i-1 i i+1 | | | | | -1/12 +1/2 -3/2 +5/6 +1/4 Upwinding prevents numerical oscillations at sharp features (e.g., near punctures). Stencil reaches 3 cells in the upwind direction -- requires ghost width >= 3. Source: finite_diff.h:90-112 | Ref: GRChombo FourthOrderDerivatives.hpp:269-301

Convergence: why 4th-order matters

With 4th-order finite differences, doubling the grid resolution (halving dx) reduces the truncation error by a factor of 2^4 = 16. This is verified by the convergence test (make test-convergence), which runs three resolutions and checks that the error ratio is ~16. If the ratio is significantly different, there is a code bug. This is the single most important diagnostic in the project.

Stride design

All stencil functions take a stride parameter instead of a direction index. This allows the same function to compute derivatives in x, y, or z by passing the appropriate stride:

Stride values for direction selection STRIDE_X = 1 x is unit-stride (innermost loop) STRIDE_Y = Ntotal one full row STRIDE_Z = Ntotal^2 one full slice f[idx + stride] steps one grid point in the chosen direction. f[idx + 2*stride] steps two grid points. And so on.

5. RK4 Time Integration

Lattice supports two Runge-Kutta methods, selected by --rk classic|ck45. Both are implemented in src/numerics/rk4.c.

Classic RK4 (4 stages, 4 memory blocks)

Classic RK4 — 4 RHS evaluations per step Memory blocks: fields (U), scratch (backup), rhs (k), accum (weighted sum) scratch = fields save initial state accum = 0 Stage 1: rhs = RHS(fields) accum += (dt/6) * rhs fields = scratch + (dt/2) * rhs half-step trial Stage 2: rhs = RHS(fields) accum += (dt/3) * rhs fields = scratch + (dt/2) * rhs half-step trial (using k2) Stage 3: rhs = RHS(fields) accum += (dt/3) * rhs fields = scratch + dt * rhs full-step trial Stage 4: rhs = RHS(fields) accum += (dt/6) * rhs fields = scratch + accum final answer Enforce algebraic constraints det(h)=1, tr(A)=0, chi>0, alpha>0

CK45 (5 stages, 3 memory blocks)

Carpenter-Kennedy 2N low-storage RK4 — 5 RHS evaluations per step Memory blocks: fields (U), scratch (dU), rhs (F) No accum needed -- saves 25% memory! dU = 0 For s = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4: F = RHS(U) dU = A[s] * dU + dt * F blend old dU with new RHS U += B[s] * dU advance solution Enforce algebraic constraints CK45 coefficients (exact rational fractions): A[0] = 0.000000 B[0] = 0.149659 Carpenter & Kennedy, NASA TM-109112 A[1] = -0.417890 B[1] = 0.379210 Solution 3 A[2] = -1.192151 B[2] = 0.822955 A[3] = -1.697784 B[3] = 0.699450 A[4] = -1.514184 B[4] = 0.153057

Memory comparison

MethodStagesMemory blocksMemory (N=128, 25 fields)
Classic RK444 (fields, scratch, rhs, accum)~6.7 GB
CK4553 (fields, scratch, rhs)~5.0 GB

CK45 is the default. It trades 25% more compute (5 vs 4 RHS evaluations) for 25% less memory — critical when N=128 already uses ~5 GB on a 16 GB Apple M4.

Algebraic enforcement

After every complete RK step, two algebraic constraints are enforced at every grid point:

  1. det(h) = 1: Rescale h_ij by 1/cbrt(det(h)) to maintain unit determinant.
  2. tr(A) = 0: Subtract (1/3) * h_ij * h^{kl} * A_{kl} from A_ij to remove any accumulated trace.

Additionally, chi and lapse are floored at 1e-12 to prevent division by zero near punctures.

6. Kreiss-Oliger Dissipation

Numerical evolution generates high-frequency noise (grid-scale oscillations) that can destabilize the simulation. Kreiss-Oliger (KO) dissipation adds a small amount of artificial viscosity that damps these oscillations without affecting the physically meaningful lower frequencies. Implemented in src/evolution/dissipation.c.

The 7-point KO stencil (6th-order)

6th-order Kreiss-Oliger dissipation stencil i-3 i-2 i-1 i i+1 i+2 i+3 | | | | | | | +1/64 -6/64 +15/64 -20/64 +15/64 -6/64 +1/64 fd_ko(f, idx, stride, dx) = (weights . stencil) / dx Applied to all 3 directions and summed: KO = sigma * (ko_x + ko_y + ko_z) This stencil approximates a 6th derivative, so it targets frequencies near dx. Source: finite_diff.h:122-136 | Ref: GRChombo FourthOrderDerivatives.hpp:361-378

The stencil reaches 3 cells in each direction, which is why the ghost zone width must be at least 3 (Lattice uses 4 to also accommodate the upwind advection stencil).

CAKO: Chi-Adjusted KO

Near a black hole puncture, chi approaches zero. Standard KO dissipation at full strength in this region can interfere with the puncture dynamics. CAKO (Chi-Adjusted Kreiss-Oliger) scales the dissipation by W = sqrt(chi), which naturally suppresses it near punctures:

sigma_eff = W * sigma_base      where W = sqrt(max(chi, 1e-10))

At the puncture center (chi ~ 0), dissipation vanishes. Far from the puncture (chi ~ 1), dissipation operates at full strength. Ref: arXiv:2404.01137, Eq. (20).

Per-field sigma

Gauge fields (lapse, shift, B) benefit from stronger dissipation than physical fields (chi, h, K, A, Theta, Gamma). When per-field sigma is enabled:

Field typeFieldsDefault sigma
Gaugelapse, shift^i, B^i0.99
Physicalchi, h_ij, K, A_ij, Theta, Gamma^i0.3

7. Sommerfeld Boundary Conditions

The wave reflection problem

A finite computational domain has boundaries. If you simply set boundary values to their initial data, outgoing gravitational waves hit the boundary and reflect back into the domain, corrupting the simulation. You need boundary conditions that let waves leave cleanly.

Radiative (Sommerfeld) boundary conditions

The Sommerfeld condition assumes all fields near the boundary behave like outgoing spherical waves plus a static asymptotic value:

Sommerfeld outgoing wave equation dt(f) = -(x^i / r) * d_i f + (f_asymptotic - f) / r ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ outgoing wave term decay-to-asymptotic term The first term propagates the field outward at the speed of light. The second term gently pushes the field toward its flat-space value at large r.

Asymptotic values

FieldAsymptotic valueReason
chi1.0Flat space has unit conformal factor
h11, h22, h331.0Flat conformal metric = identity
lapse1.0Flat space has unit lapse
All others0.0K, A_ij, Theta, Gamma, shift, B, off-diag h all vanish in flat space

Block-aware variant

For AMR multi-block meshes, apply_sommerfeld_block() only applies Sommerfeld BCs to ghost points adjacent to actual domain boundaries (where on_boundary[face] == 1). Ghost zones shared with neighboring blocks are filled by the ghost exchange instead. Physical coordinates use BLOCK_COORD() to account for the block's position in the global domain.

8. Moving Puncture Gauge

The lapse and shift are not determined by Einstein's equations — they represent coordinate freedom. The "moving puncture" gauge choice is the standard for black hole simulations.

Bona-Masso lapse (1+log slicing)

dt(alpha) = advec_coeff * beta^i d_i alpha
          - c * alpha^p * (K - 2*Theta)

With defaults c = 2.0 and p = 1.0, this gives "1+log" slicing. The lapse collapses (alpha approaches 0) in regions of strong curvature, naturally avoiding the singularity inside a black hole. Near a Schwarzschild puncture, the lapse settles to the "trumpet" profile with alpha ~ 0.3 at the puncture center.

Gamma-driver shift

dt(beta^i) = advec_coeff * beta^j d_j beta^i + F * B^i
dt(B^i)    = advec_coeff * (beta^j d_j B^i - beta^j d_j Gamma^i) + dt(Gamma^i) - eta * B^i

The Gamma-driver shift makes the coordinates co-move with the puncture, preventing the coordinate singularity from running away. The damping parameter eta = 1.0 prevents the shift from oscillating. The auxiliary variable B^i provides the necessary memory for this second-order-in-time evolution.

SSL: Slow-Start Lapse

At the start of a simulation, the initial lapse (alpha = sqrt(chi)) is not the true trumpet solution. The gauge dynamics produce a large initial pulse as the lapse adjusts. SSL adds a temporary Gaussian damping term that drives the lapse toward W = sqrt(chi) during early evolution, then decays to zero:

rhs_lapse += -W * h_ssl * exp(-t^2 / (2*sigma_t^2)) * (alpha - W)

This damps the initial gauge wave, allowing a cleaner start. After t ~ 100M, the exponential factor makes this term negligible. Ref: arXiv:2404.01137, Eq. (27).

Source: ccz4_rhs.c:398-425  |  Ref: GRChombo MovingPunctureGauge.hpp:54-65

9. Puncture Initial Data

A black hole is represented as a "puncture" — a point where the conformal factor diverges (chi goes to zero). Lattice implements Brill-Lindquist initial data for N non-spinning, uncharged black holes at rest. Source: src/initial_data/puncture.c.

Brill-Lindquist conformal factor

Brill-Lindquist: psi = 1 + sum_n M_n / (2 r_n) For a single BH with mass M at the origin: psi(r) = 1 + M / (2r) chi = 1 / psi^4 chi 1 |............................. | 0.5 | . | . 0 |__._________________________ 0 M/2 2M 4M r chi approaches 0 at the puncture (r=0), reaches 1 far from the BH. The conformal metric h_ij = delta_ij (flat). Physical metric gamma_ij = psi^4 * delta_ij = delta_ij / chi.

Why lapse = sqrt(chi)?

The "obvious" choice alpha = 1 everywhere produces a violent initial gauge pulse as the lapse rapidly collapses near the puncture. Setting alpha = sqrt(chi) = 1/psi^2 provides a pre-collapsed lapse that is already close to the trumpet solution. This dramatically reduces initial transients and lets the simulation settle to the physical solution faster. Combined with SSL, this gives a clean start for single-BH evolutions.

10. Constraints

The Hamiltonian and momentum constraints are "checksums" on the simulation. If the numerical solution satisfies Einstein's equations perfectly, these constraints vanish identically. In practice, they measure how much numerical error has accumulated.

Hamiltonian constraint

H = R + (2/3)*K^2 - A^{ij}*A_{ij}

This uses the Ricci scalar with Z = 0 (the pure geometric Ricci, not the CCZ4 Ricci with Z terms). This matches GRChombo's NewConstraints implementation, which calls compute_ricci without Z contributions.

Momentum constraint

M_i = -(2/3)*d_i K + h^{jk}*covd_k A_{ji} - (3/(2*chi))*h^{jk}*A_{ij}*d_k chi

The covariant derivative of A includes Christoffel symbol corrections: covd_k A_{ij} = d_k A_{ij} - Gamma^l_{ki} A_{lj} - Gamma^l_{kj} A_{li}.

L2 norm monitoring

The constraint violation is measured as an L2 (root-mean-square) norm over the interior grid points:

L2(H) = sqrt( sum(H^2) / N_points )
L2(M) = sqrt( sum(|M|^2) / (3 * N_points) )

For the flat spacetime test, L2(H) must remain below 1e-10 after 1000 steps. For a single Schwarzschild puncture, constraints should converge at 4th order (16x reduction when doubling resolution).

Source: src/diagnostics/constraints.c  |  Ref: GRChombo NewConstraints.impl.hpp:55-100

11. Psi4 / Gravitational Waves

Psi4 (the Newman-Penrose Weyl scalar) encodes the outgoing gravitational wave content of the spacetime. It is the primary observable for comparing numerical relativity simulations with gravitational wave detector data.

What Psi4 measures

Psi4 is a complex scalar constructed from the Weyl curvature tensor projected onto a null tetrad (a set of four null vectors adapted to the outgoing radiation direction). At large distances from the source, Psi4 is directly related to the gravitational wave strain: h_+ - i*h_x = integral(integral(Psi4)).

Extraction procedure

The standard approach is to extract Psi4 on a coordinate sphere at a fixed extraction radius r_ex (typically 50-100M), decompose it into spin-weighted spherical harmonics Y^{-2}_{lm}, and record the dominant (l=2, m=2) mode as a function of time. This gives the gravitational waveform.

Not yet implemented. Psi4 extraction is planned for Phase 1, Milestone 5 (binary inspiral). The file src/diagnostics/psi4.c does not yet exist. The GRChombo reference for implementation is Source/CCZ4/Weyl4.impl.hpp.
